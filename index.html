What a guide contains

  • A page map drawn to scale, so you can see how much of the file is endnotes before you start.
  • Every argument narrated, in stages, with the terms defined where they appear.
  • Real examples for the abstract claims, with names, dates and what they cost.
  • A glossary at the end, grouped by text.
  • Notes, highlights and comments you write yourself, saved in your browser.

About what you write

Your notes, highlights and comments stay in your own browser. They are saved on the device you are using and they survive a reload, a closed tab and a restart.

They do not travel to another computer or another browser, they are never sent anywhere, and nobody else can see them. Clearing your browsing data for this site removes them. A private window keeps them only until you close it. Each guide has an export button that saves everything to a file, and an import button that reads it back, which is how you move your work to another machine or keep a backup.

One thing to expect. These guides get rewritten often, whenever a better way to answer a question turns up. A highlight is anchored to the words around it, so it survives edits elsewhere on the page. If the exact paragraph you marked gets rewritten, that highlight goes. Sorry. It is the price of the guide getting better.
